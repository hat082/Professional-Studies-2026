## Submit

RoadSense is an IoT package which includes a multi-modal sensor device and mobile software. It collects road condition information via LiDAR, multi-camera fusion and full 360 radar arrays to produce alerts on pot-hole abundant roads via road condition detection as well as providing 360 mapping for providing environmental perception of the surrounding objects. This technology has the potential to significantly reduce fatalities and injuries of cyclists in the Netherlands as the country experiences the greatest number of traffic-related deaths from accidents involving bicycles (36%) [[1]](https://swov.nl/en/fact-sheet/road-deaths-netherlands).

The software integrates irregularities of the local roads within open-source mapping and provides information to the consumers about non-ideal/dangerous road conditions and suggests safest routes to destinations. The software also provides views of the surrounding area to provide information on blind spots.

Moreover, RoadSense could improve traffic-security for cyclists and car drivers, as cyclists can make better traffic decisions from the software, and cars can expect more predictable behaviors from the cyclists. This product is innovative and original as there are no standardised environmental perception systems for bicycles as a commercial product. There are academic prototypes such as the RoadSense [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1145/3714394.3756286), instrumented bicycles [[3]](https://transp-or.epfl.ch/heart/2024/abstracts/hEART_2024_paper_7406.pdf), but they lack state-of-the-art sensors and does not perform surrounding vehicles sensing and road condition detection simultaneously.

The business model is founded upon selling subscription-based IoT platform and DaaS to three major targets: cyclists, bicycle manufacturers, insurance companies and the government. Partnerships with large organisations and a subscription-based model guarantees stable and constant stream of income for investors and stakeholders.

## Feedback

I think you are proposing that the cyclist has the Lidar system rather than cars. Car based Lidar will have many more competitors. If it's cycle based then there is more scope for filing patents, if it is car based then you will need to consider your USP. Assuming cycle based, consider the following points:

1. **Design** - how compact can you made this to ensure a good experience
2. **Market** - Will you consider selling the technology to personal cyclists first or would you consider e-bike companies?
3. **Branding** - The name seems descriptive/obvious so you may find it difficult to obtain a trademark. Check the Nice classification database but I think you may need to consider an alternative.
